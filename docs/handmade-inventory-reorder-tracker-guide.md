## The basic reorder point

A simple planning rule is:

`reorder point = average daily usage × supplier lead time + safety stock`

Example: if you use 3 units per day, delivery normally takes 10 days and you want 10 units of safety stock, the reorder point is 40 units.

That means the signal appears before stock reaches zero. The purpose is to give the next order enough time to arrive while you continue selling or producing.

## What a handmade inventory tracker should record

For each important material or finished SKU, keep at least:

- item/SKU name,
- current quantity,
- average usage or sales rate,
- supplier lead time,
- safety stock,
- reorder point,
- unit cost,
- last restock date.

For makers producing in batches, it also helps to separate raw materials from finished goods. A candle maker can have enough wax but too few finished units, while a jewelry seller can have finished pieces but be short on one finding needed for the next batch.

## Avoid two common mistakes

**Reordering only when stock is nearly zero.** This ignores lead time and often creates emergency purchases.

**Using the same safety stock for every item.** A stable, easy-to-source item may need less buffer than a bestseller with unpredictable demand or a slow supplier.

## A five-minute weekly routine

Once a week:

1. update quantities for your fastest-moving items,
2. compare current stock with the reorder point,
3. review whether daily usage has changed,
4. update supplier lead times if recent deliveries were slower or faster,
5. reorder only where the numbers justify it.

This keeps inventory decisions tied to actual usage instead of memory.
