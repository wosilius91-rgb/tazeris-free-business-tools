# Maker Market Sales, Inventory & Pricing Guide

A craft fair or maker market can look profitable while still hiding weak margins, slow-moving stock, and products that need a price change. A simple event worksheet is useful when it connects **sales, unit cost, inventory, fees, and restock decisions** instead of tracking revenue alone.

## 1. Start with true unit cost

For each product, include the costs that actually scale with a sale:

- materials and components
- packaging
- payment or marketplace fees
- direct labor you want included in product economics
- any other per-unit cost that changes with each item sold

A basic contribution profit calculation is:

`unit contribution = selling price - unit variable cost - per-sale fees`

Do not confuse contribution profit with final business profit. Booth fees, travel, insurance, subscriptions, taxes, and other overhead may still need to be deducted separately.

## 2. Measure event-level profit

For one market, record:

- opening inventory by SKU
- units sold
- selling price actually received
- discounts
- payment fees
- booth fee
- travel and event-specific costs
- closing inventory

A simplified event result is:

`event profit = sales - product costs - payment fees - booth fee - travel - other event costs`

## 3. Track sell-through, not just sales

A product that sells 12 units sounds better than one that sells 6, but that comparison is incomplete if you brought 100 units of the first product and only 8 of the second.

Use:

`sell-through % = units sold / units available × 100`

High sell-through can signal that the item deserves more stock next time. Low sell-through may mean the price, product mix, display, season, or event audience needs attention.

## 4. Separate pricing decisions from restock decisions

A strong seller can still have a weak margin. A high-margin item can still sell too slowly to deserve more shelf space. Keep both signals visible:

- **margin signal** — is the selling price high enough after direct costs and fees?
- **demand signal** — how quickly does the item sell?
- **stock signal** — will current stock cover expected demand before the next production or supplier cycle?

For a simplified reorder calculation, use the free [Reorder Point Calculator](https://tazeris-money-factory-production.up.railway.app/free/reorder-point-calculator?utm_source=github&utm_medium=owned&utm_campaign=maker_market_guide).

## 5. Compare events consistently

Use the same definitions from event to event. If labor is included in product cost for one market but omitted for another, comparisons become misleading. Keep consistent columns for:

- gross sales
- discounts
- fees
- product cost
- event-specific cost
- profit
- units sold
- sell-through
- closing stock

After several events, compare which products and market types generate the strongest **profit per event**, not only the highest revenue.
