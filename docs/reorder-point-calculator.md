# Reorder point planning for small product businesses

A reorder point is the stock level at which you begin replenishment so normal demand can continue while you wait for the supplier or production process. A common planning rule is:

`reorder point = average daily demand × lead time + safety stock`

This is intentionally simple. It is useful when you want a quick operational trigger, but it does not predict future demand or replace a full inventory plan.

## Example

If a product normally uses or sells 3 units per day, replenishment takes 10 days, and you keep 10 units as safety stock, the reorder point is 40 units. When available stock approaches that level, it is time to review whether a new order or production batch should start.

The quality of the result depends on the inputs. Use a realistic average demand period, include the full supplier or production lead time, and set safety stock based on actual uncertainty rather than an arbitrary large buffer. Seasonal demand, supplier delays, minimum order quantities, damaged inventory and cash constraints can all require a more detailed decision.
