## What to enter

A practical pricing check should include:

1. Material or production cost per item.
2. Packaging and other per-order costs.
3. Labor cost or the value of your time.
4. Your expected selling price.
5. Marketplace/payment-fee assumptions that apply to your shop.
6. Any other costs you want included in the estimate.

Fees can vary by country, payment setup, ads and other conditions, so the calculator keeps assumptions visible rather than pretending one percentage fits every seller.

## Example

Suppose one product costs 8.00 to make, packaging adds 1.50, labor adds 7.50 and you are considering a 32.00 selling price. The important result is not revenue alone. You need to see what remains after the costs and fee assumptions you entered, then compare that profit with the margin you actually want.

Run several scenarios before launching a sale. A discount that looks small to a customer can remove a large share of seller profit when the original margin is already thin.

## A simple pricing routine

Before you publish a new Etsy product:

- calculate the full unit cost,
- add labor instead of treating your time as free,
- test the expected fee assumptions,
- check the resulting profit and margin,
- test at least one discount scenario,
- save the assumptions you used so you can review them later.

That process is more useful than copying a competitor’s price because two sellers can have very different material costs, labor time, shipping structure and fee exposure.
