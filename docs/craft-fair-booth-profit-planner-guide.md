# Craft Fair Booth Profit Planner: Know Whether a Market Was Worth It

A busy craft fair can feel successful while still producing weak profit. Sales alone do not tell you whether the booth fee, product costs, travel and payment fees left enough money to justify the event.

## Costs to include

For a simple event-profit check, enter:

- total event sales,
- product cost for what was sold,
- booth/vendor fee,
- travel or parking,
- other event costs,
- payment-processing percentage.

If you have other meaningful costs, include them in your own event record too. The goal is to avoid treating gross sales as profit.

## Example

Suppose a market produces 600 in sales. Product costs are 180, booth fee is 80, travel is 35, other costs are 20 and payment fees average 2.5% of sales. That leaves 270 before costs or taxes you did not enter.

The number becomes much more useful when compared across events. A smaller local market can outperform a larger event if its booth fee, travel time and inventory requirements are lower.

## Plan the event before you go

A useful pre-event check answers three questions:

1. How much do I need to sell just to cover event-specific costs?
2. What sales level would make the day worth my time?
3. How much inventory should I bring without massively overproducing?

After the event, record the actual result and compare it with the plan. Over several markets, this builds evidence about which event types, locations and product mixes deserve more inventory.

## A repeatable post-market review

At the end of every event:

- record final sales,
- count remaining inventory,
- total booth/travel/other costs,
- estimate payment fees,
- calculate event profit,
- note the top-selling products,
- note what sold out too early and what barely moved.

This turns every craft fair into data for the next one instead of relying on memory.
