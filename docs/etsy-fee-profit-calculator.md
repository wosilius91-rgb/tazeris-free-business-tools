# Etsy fee, profit and target-price planning

This short guide is for sellers who want to estimate what one Etsy order may leave after seller fees and business costs. It is a planning example, not accounting, tax or legal advice. Etsy charges and payment-processing details can vary by country, currency, account and transaction, so verify the rates that actually apply to your shop.

## A useful order-level model

Start with item revenue plus any shipping charged to the buyer. Then subtract the costs that belong to the order: materials or product cost, packaging, direct labor, shipping you pay, and any overhead you intentionally allocate to the order. Separately account for listing or renewal fees, transaction fees, payment-processing fees and any applicable advertising-related percentage.

A simple example: if an item sells for 30, the buyer pays 5 shipping, and your direct business costs total 20, the order has 35 of revenue before seller fees. The remaining profit depends on the actual percentage and fixed fees that apply. This is why a calculator with editable fee fields is safer than copying one universal percentage from another seller.

## Work backwards from a target margin

The same inputs can also be used backwards: choose the profit margin you want, keep the percentage-based fees in the equation, and solve for the item price that could support the target. If the requested margin plus percentage fees leaves too little room for the entered fixed costs, the target may be mathematically infeasible at that cost structure.
