# Craft fair profit planning

A busy event can feel successful because sales are visible, but the useful business question is what remains after the costs required to attend and fulfill those sales. A simple event-level view starts with total sales and subtracts product cost, booth fee, travel, other event costs and payment-processing fees.

## Example

Suppose an event produces 600 in sales. Product cost is 180, booth fee is 80, travel is 35, other event costs are 20, and card-processing fees average 2.5% of sales. The payment fee is 15, total entered costs are 330, and the remaining event profit is 270 before any costs or taxes not entered. That is a 45% margin on sales for the inputs used.

This quick model becomes more useful when you compare events consistently. Record the same cost categories every time, note remaining inventory, and avoid treating revenue as profit. If an event requires several days of preparation or staffing, include the labor or operating cost in a fuller analysis even if it is not part of the quick calculator.
