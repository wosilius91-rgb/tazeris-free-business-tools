# True Margin Monitoring for Small Product Businesses

Revenue can rise while profit falls. The common reason is that prices, supplier costs, payment fees, shipping, discounts, and channel fees change at different times. A useful margin monitor keeps the calculation simple enough to update often and detailed enough to catch erosion before it becomes expensive.

## 1. Define the margin you actually want to watch

For operational decisions, a practical starting point is contribution margin:

`contribution = net selling price - unit cost - variable fees - variable fulfillment cost`

`contribution margin % = contribution / net selling price × 100`

Net selling price should reflect the price actually collected after discounts. Unit cost should use the current cost you expect to replace the item at when that is more useful than an old historical cost.

## 2. Include channel-specific fees

The same product can have a different real margin on different channels. Keep separate assumptions for costs such as:

- payment processing
- marketplace transaction fees
- listing or referral fees when applicable
- shipping subsidies paid by the seller
- packaging
- discount codes
- returns or refund allowance when material

Do not copy one margin percentage across every channel unless the cost structure is genuinely identical.

## 3. Watch changes, not only the current number

A monitor becomes more useful when it flags movement. Examples:

- supplier cost rose by 8%
- payment fee changed
- average discount increased
- shipping cost increased
- selling price stayed flat while variable cost rose

For each SKU or service, keep the current margin and the previous margin. Then calculate:

`margin change = current margin % - previous margin %`

A falling margin is not automatically a problem, but it should trigger a review before more volume amplifies the loss.

## 4. Use thresholds as review signals

Instead of assuming one universal “good” margin, define thresholds that fit the product and business model. A simple operating setup can use:

- target margin
- review threshold
- minimum acceptable contribution

When a product falls below its review threshold, check the cause before changing price automatically. The problem could be cost, discounting, fees, or a temporary shipping change.

## 5. Test a price before changing it

If the current margin is too low, calculate the price needed for a target margin rather than guessing.

A simplified form is:

`required price = variable cost / (1 - target margin rate - percentage fee rate)`

The real formula can differ when there are fixed per-order fees, taxes, shipping rules, or multiple items per order. Treat the result as a planning estimate and test it against customer demand.

## 6. Review on a useful cadence

For stable products, a monthly check may be enough. For products with volatile supplier costs, heavy discounting, or multiple marketplaces, review more often. Focus attention where inputs actually change instead of manually rechecking every item every day.
